What round 1 taught us, and what round 2 delivers

Round 1 proved the concept: on real accounts, Insights Forge produced customer-ready engagement plans — U-Haul being the reference. It also showed why results varied: quality depended on who was driving and how they asked. Round 2 fixes that systematically. Every piece of pilot feedback traces to a specific, verified change — and nothing has been modified yet. Implementation starts on approval.

Three findings from round 1

Good results required an experienced driver. Open-ended intake meant experienced analysts fed the tool well and newer ones didn't — and thin input produced thin plans without pushback.
The tool's standards weren't enforced. The brand and quality rules existed all along, but applying them was voluntary. Deliverables produced off the intended path skipped every check — that's where the inconsistency came from.
Hours were invested before direction was confirmed. With no lightweight "is this right?" checkpoint, an early wrong turn cost an afternoon instead of a minute.

What round 2 delivers

Same inputs, same quality — regardless of who's driving
A structured intake form generates the engagement brief, the agent challenges thin answers instead of accepting them, and the analyst's experience and account familiarity calibrate how it explains and probes. New analysts get more guidance; experts get less friction.
Ben & Julie's consistency asks · Angie's form advantage · Paul's variance question
Direction confirmed before investment
Every engagement passes a Direction Check the analyst approves before deep analysis begins, and the agent asks focused questions at defined points so account knowledge shapes the work early instead of correcting it late.
Ben's confirm-before-assets ask · the wrong-direction failure mode
One deliverable, done to one standard
Exactly what was agreed: a 30/60/90-day Customer Action Plan — every gap cited to evidence, every action sequenced — as an executive one-pager with optional deck. Quality rules now apply automatically, and the deck is inspected before it's ever called done.
Jeremy's narrowed scope · Angie's deck-quality complaint
Trustworthy by construction
Every claim carries its evidence status — measured from client telemetry, reported by a named stakeholder, or an industry assumption — and confidence scores anchor to it. Recommendations outside agreed scope need the analyst's explicit yes. Any reviewer can judge any analyst's output by one standard.
Barnes' scope drift · Chase's trust concern · Ben's sign-off criterion
The analyst stays in control — and moves faster
One command starts or re-enters any engagement at the right point. The analyst chooses which findings the plan focuses on, can accept the review panel's first pass, and gets a short reading list at each checkpoint. Client pushback after delivery reopens the engagement cleanly.
Julie's "less, faster" · Wil's "more detailed than needed" · Barnes' management burden
Ready to operate as a team
A local viewer lets a manager review any engagement's artifacts as they're produced; session notes and a complete reasoning record are captured automatically; documentation is rewritten in plain English for newer analysts; and the model configuration now tracks current releases so the system never falls a generation behind again.

Three asks of leadership

1Ratify the plan at the team review. Twenty-five decisions are teed up with recommendations — mostly confirmations of direction already discussed, including manager sign-off before customer-facing output.
2Keep round 2 with the same cohort. The experienced group proves the consistency features; the newer-analyst rollout is staged behind it with onboarding materials built into this plan.
3Progress the org-level decisions in parallel. Licensing, data governance, and Salesforce/Slack integration sit deliberately outside this plan — it doesn't block on them, and the integration groundwork is already laid.
Measure of success, unchanged: Ben's round-1 criterion — would we sign off on this output for any analyst? Round 2 runs the same engagement flow, same cohort, with the variance sources closed.
