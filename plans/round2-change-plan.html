StoryBrand restructure — one-pager and deck as the customer's story

The one-pager becomes the SB7 arc — the customer (hero) has a Problem → meets a Guide (Insights + Dynatrace) → who gives them a Plan → with clear Stakes → and one Decision to make. The retro-fit test against the real U-Haul one-pager is the evidence: five of six beats are already latent in its content (the Guide beat fits best of all — the findings' teal/blue/magenta color semantics already encode evidence tiers), and the one missing beat, Success, is a genuine gap — the transformation exists only as verb clauses buried in action rows, and one of them ("Converts Dynatrace from a reporting tool to an operational platform") has the platform as subject, which is exactly what the hero rule rewrites ("Amy's team stops filing analytics requests and starts receiving automatic alerts"). Bonus: the Executive Sponsor archetype's own question list maps one-to-one onto the beats — including "what happens if we do nothing for 30 days?", which the current structure only answers by accident.

  • Five printed sections, seven beats — the beats are an authoring skeleton, not printed labels (a heading reading "The Guide" is marketing jargon to exactly the reader Mike Chase worried about): The situation (Character + Problem, internal-problem sentence only if sourced — never composed) → What we found (Guide: one empathy lead + 3–5 tier-tagged findings; the tiers are the authority mechanism) → The plan (exactly the three rollup rows, tradeoffs inline) → What's at stake (Failure + Success compressed into one day-90 contrast block — separate sections invite padded doom; the contrast form forces both sides to the same horizon) → The decision (direct + transitional CTA). Word arithmetic verified: 485–630 vs. the U-Haul artifact's ~650 — the one-page constraint holds.
  • The hero rule, made mechanical: the client is the grammatical subject of Problem and Success sentences; the platform may be subject only in Guide and Plan. It's fidelity-gate check 6, not a taste note — plus the new "Guide-as-hero" pitfall: a one-pager where the platform stars in every sentence is a product pitch wearing a consulting deliverable's clothes.
  • Evidence-honest stakes: a Failure statement must be a measured trajectory, a client-reported concern (named stakeholder), or an explicitly-labeled industry pattern — never an invented prediction. Success statements are reads of the rollup's outcome column, already tiered at Phase 2. On the deck, the success side renders royal blue, not teal — teal means confirmed, and coloring planned outcomes in the confirmed color would be tier-laundering by palette.
  • The fidelity gate grows to six checks (Plan↔rollup order · Success↔outcome column · Failure↔named risks/trajectories with tiers intact · CTA↔decision asks, no invented micro-asks · findings keep tiers, open reads open · hero rule + business-impact opening), because Phase 3 runs no lens — the gate is the only backstop for the new authored prose, and every new sentence must be a restatement of pre-approved content.
  • Deck arc re-sequenced, machinery untouched: 8 slides (title → situation → findings → the WS-3 30/60/90 three-column slide, repositioned → the new stakes-contrast slide → pure-ask decision slide → appendix → closing); brand rules, generator, layout bindings, fonts all unchanged.
  • What this deliberately does not touch: action-plan.md stays Gap→Solution→30/60/90 — the fidelity gate needs a stable analytical source of truth, and narrativizing it would mean checking a story against a story. One analysis artifact, one narrative artifact, one gate between them. Personas re-tone but never reorder the arc (practitioners get a one-clause empathy lead and SLI-framed stakes; reordering would fork the gate and the deck table).
One deliberate deviation from the book (D29): Miller's canonical order puts the call-to-action before failure/success; this spec puts Stakes at section 4 and the Decision last, preserving the repo's existing "end with the specific decision being requested" rule — the last thing the exec reads is the approval, not the doom. Standard SB7 collateral variant, but since "StoryBrand framework" was the directive, the variance is flagged for sign-off.
