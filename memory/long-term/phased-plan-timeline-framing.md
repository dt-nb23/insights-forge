# Phased plan timeline framing

## The 30/60/90 convention

Frame every action plan in three windows:

- **30 days** — what the team can confirm, stand up, or ship inside one month. First-look results, quick validations, instrumentation gaps closed.
- **60 days** — what lands in the second month, usually after the 30-day findings gate and a one-sprint implementation cycle.
- **90 days** — the full picture. Steady-state measurement, escalation paths resolved, leadership review-ready.

Ninety days is the maximum end-to-end horizon for an engagement plan. If work cannot close in 90 days, it belongs in a separate follow-on engagement, not appended to this one.

## Day framing is the presentation layer

Thirty, sixty, and ninety days are the *executive presentation layer* over underlying week-range estimates. Underneath each window, the team works in sprint-sized increments. Use day framing in one-pagers and decks; use week-range estimates in action-plan.md.

| Presentation layer | What it maps to |
|---|---|
| 30 days | Weeks 1–4 (days 1–30) |
| 60 days | Weeks 5–8 (days 31–60) |
| 90 days | Weeks 9–13 (days 61–90) |

## Phase naming on the one-pager

In Phase 03 of the one-pager, name the three windows as phases, not as calendar dates:

- **Phase 1 — Confirm (30 days):** [what gets validated]
- **Phase 2 — Improve (60 days):** [what gets shipped or tuned]
- **Phase 3 — Measure (90 days):** [what gets quantified and presented]

The phase names should reflect what the work achieves, not just when it happens. "Confirm," "Improve," and "Measure" are defaults — substitute names that match the engagement's narrative.

## Concurrency note

Always include a sentence explaining which phases can overlap and which must be sequential. Example: "Phase 2 instrumentation work can begin in parallel with Phase 1 validation — both teams do not need to wait." Without a concurrency note, a reader assumes all phases are sequential and will overestimate calendar time.
